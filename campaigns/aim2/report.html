Is Aim 2 powered?

Simulated level and power for the latent mediation test, on the improved design with the family-history recruitment route.

The answer

No — not at 80 per cent, anywhere in the plausible range. The best case is 55 per cent, and the direct-path test is weaker still.

Every null cell holds its level, so these figures are readable rather than an artefact of a broken test. What they say is that 1,600 people detects mediation about half the time when the mediated effect is at the top of the range considered, and detects a direct path almost never.

Level first

A power figure read off a test that over-rejects is not a power figure, so the null cells were run at the same replicate count as the alternatives and read before anything else. The vertical estimand is a product, so its null is a union — the loading is nought, or the path is — and a test can hold against one branch and not the other. Both branches are here, and so is the direct-path null, which the first campaign never measured at all.

The last two rows are the adversarial ones. They set the direct path to nought while the mediated effect is real and large. A direct-path test that borrowed evidence from the mediated path would over-reject there. It sits at nominal, so it does not.

The loading-nought cell is conservative by construction. With the loading on its boundary the reference is simulated rather than assumed, and at 50 draws the smallest p-value it can return is near 0.02 — so rejection needs none of 50 draws to exceed. That is why it comes in at 0.020, and it means the vertical power below is, if anything, slightly understated.

What the design buys

Power against the mediated effect θv = ab, in two measurement arms. Probands either give a binary, misclassified ABR — the protocol for people too impaired for behavioural audiometry — or a full audiogram. The pair brackets the severity question without having to know what fraction falls each way.

Reaching 80 per cent at the most favourable effect needs roughly 1.7× the sample with audiograms (about 2,700 people) or 2.1× with ABR alone (about 3,350). The multiplier comes from the non-centrality implied by each observed power, which assumes the likelihood ratio behaves as a chi-square — reasonable away from the union null, and stated here as the approximation it is.

Audiograms are worth about 1.2× more per person, not the 2.7× the small-effect cells appear to show. At θv = 0.12 the ABR arm's power is 0.070 ± 0.018, far too imprecise to invert into a non-centrality; the apparent advantage there is noise. At 0.24, where power is measured well, the real advantage is modest. Widening audiometry coverage helps, but adding people is the lever that closes the gap.

The finding that matters more than the power

Across every cell where the true direct path is 0.2 — comparable in size to the mediated effect it is meant to be told apart from — the direct-path test rejects between 0.06 and 0.12. That is close to no power at all, so a second grid was run to ask what size of direct path would be detectable. The answer is: a much larger one than the model is likely to carry.

Even a direct path of 0.7 reaches only 55 per cent, and 80 per cent is not reached anywhere. The two arms land on a comparison that states the asymmetry exactly: with audiograms, a direct path of 0.7 gives 0.545 — the same power, to three decimals, as a mediated effect of 0.24. The two estimands are equally detectable only when the direct one is about 2.9 times larger. Audiograms barely help either, which fits: the weakness is identification, not measurement precision.

Aim 2's central claim is separating vertical from horizontal pleiotropy. On these numbers the study detects mediation about half the time and would detect a direct path only if it were very large, which means a null result on that path would carry almost no information. The family-count ladder of 18 August already found this path weakly identified; what is new is a number on it.

That is what the horizontal confidence set is for. Where the likelihood falls away in neither direction the set reports itself unbounded rather than pretending to a value, which is the Strategy's existing hedge — not reassigning the horizontal alpha when the path is not separately estimable — made operational. On this evidence the hedge is not merely prudent, and it is worth asking whether the direct path should be offered as a testable claim at all rather than as an estimand reported with a set that may be open at both ends.

What stands behind the numbers

  • Real families, not synthetic ones. 671 units from the Acoustic pedigrees and the family-history route, with age-specific dementia risk rather than one rate for everybody; every status observed, since a fifty-year-old with a clean CDR is a known non-case.
  • Ascertainment cannot bias the estimands. Recruiting through an affected proband contributes exactly families × log(1/prevalence) to the log likelihood — verified at four prevalences — and a constant cannot move a maximum. It holds for one named proband with a threshold fixed from known prevalence, and would not if the threshold were estimated.
  • The fast integrator agrees with the accurate one, and does not drift as families grow. Sequential truncation against quasi-Monte Carlo on fitted estimands and p-values, at the sizes the design actually contains. The worry was that an approximation conditioning one dimension at a time would decay in larger families. It does not: the disagreement shows no trend with size, and the worst of it is at size three, not at size eight. On the mediated estimand it runs 1×10−2 at size three (about 4 per cent of one standard error), and 1×10−5 at eight, the largest unit in the design — a tenth of one per cent of a standard error at six, and none of one at eight. The sharpest case is at size eight, where the mediation p-value lands at 0.0195, below the .025 threshold and therefore on the rejecting side: the two routes give 0.019529 and 0.019582, so they agree on the decision and not merely on a number, by a hundredth of the distance to the threshold. Every difference is far below the ±.011 Monte Carlo noise these rejection rates already carry. It runs 300–350× faster (590 s against 2 s at size eight), which is what makes the real pedigrees usable at all.
  • Refusals are counted, never dropped. Between 2 and 7 replicates per cell of 200; they stay in the denominator, so a refusal counts against power rather than quietly improving it.

What would change these numbers

Six changes were run on the same design, each altering exactly one thing and each carrying its own null cell under the identical change, so a lever that lifted power by lifting rejections generally would show up rather than be credited. Every level held.

It is not more people. It is which people, and how they are graded. Twice the adjudicated families reaches 80 per cent at 2,000 people, where recruiting half again as many of everybody reaches 71 at 2,369 — three hundred and sixty-nine fewer people for thirteen more points. Staging the outcome by CDR buys fourteen points on the same 1,600 people and the same 256 cases, which makes it the cheapest change available: nobody extra is recruited and nothing is measured that is not measured already.

The two that bought nothing say as much as the one that bought most. Sharpening the hearing measurement moved power 0.065, barely outside the noise, because it improves the loading and the loading was never what was short. Dropping the 185 people in Acoustic-only units cost nothing measurable at all.

One fact explains every row. The mediation signal lives in the hearing-to-dementia covariance across relatives. A lone case contributes almost none of it; a case with two offspring contributes several informative pairs. That is why doubling ascertained families beats recruiting broadly, why the offspring sweep found two offspring per case beating 250 lone cases despite sixty-six fewer adjudications, and why people who bring no relatives and never become cases are close to inert for this aim.

In information terms staging is 1.34 times and doubling the adjudicated families 2.02, against the 1.81 that 80 per cent needs. Staging with half again as many adjudicated families would reach it at 1,800 people. Those ratios multiply only if the two act on independent parts of the information, which is plausible — one changes who is enrolled, the other how they are graded — but it is arithmetic rather than a measurement, and the single-change figures above are the measured ones.

Nothing here rescues the direct-path test. Its weakness is identification rather than sample size, so more people improve it only slowly. What would break it is an instrument — a variant affecting hearing but not dementia except through hearing — which is what Mendelian randomisation is for and belongs in a different model.
